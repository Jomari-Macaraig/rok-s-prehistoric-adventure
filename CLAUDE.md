# Rok Runner

Godot 4.7 (GDScript, Compatibility renderer) 2D endless runner with a prehistoric theme, built as a landscape mobile game. Rok, a caveman, flees a T-rex (it roars at the start of every run and bites if stumbles let it catch up), runs automatically and shapeshifts into a Raptor, a Triceratops, a Spinosaurus or a flying Pteranodon to clear obstacles. Touch controls: five form buttons bottom-left, SLIDE and JUMP bottom-right, pause, sound and Field Guide top-left. The run is a slow curriculum: each hazard is introduced alone with a hint, then mixed into combos. Dinosaurs unlock by best distance, and the Field Guide encyclopedia reveals what you've unlocked and learned (with real-world facts about each animal). Meat collected in runs is banked and spent in the Market on power-ups, the third booster slot and 28 expansion dinosaurs. Each dinosaur belongs to a category (Runner, Smasher, Tank, Flyer: one per dino button, 8 dinosaurs each), and an owned one can be equipped in the Herd screen (which compares attributes side by side) to replace that button's dinosaur, with its own art and trade-off attributes. Market dinosaurs come in tiers per category (Starter, Skilled, Expert, Legend): higher tiers need that category's own badge, earned by playing it, and power-ups each arrive before the stretch they help with (some behind a matching badge). Besides Rok, the heroes Nira (scout) and Boro (guardian) can be bought in the Market and played in his place. Rok has 3 hearts (Heart Containers, sold in the Market once your best score is high enough, add up to 3 more): crashing into an obstacle costs hearts by its damage tier (`config/damage.cfg`: a quarter for small rocks, half for logs, a whole heart for walls; Armor softens it) instead of ending the run, while pits, liquids, quicksand, dino gates and being caught are still instant; rare heart pickups heal. Each run ends on a summary card with a steep score rank (C to SS) whose multiplier boosts that run's meat and dinosaur XP; dinosaurs earn XP by the metres Rok runs as them and need it (plus meat) to level up (1–10) in the Herd. Tiered badges (Bronze/Silver/Gold, paid in meat) track long-term goals in the Field Guide, a flag marks your best distance in every run, each zone has its own hunter (T-rex, Carnotaurus, Allosaurus) that charges at set distances, and wild dinosaurs (a different land and flying pair per zone; some flyers come in high and dive at him) run at Rok, to be dodged and then eaten by the T-rex behind him. Long runs stay lively: clearing hazards without a stumble builds a combo streak (up to ×5 points) and fills a meter for Primal Frenzy, a few seconds of smashing through everything; a chest waits every 1000 m (common, rare, ultra rare or legendary, each with its own art, rarer the further you go); meat turns golden and rare fossils appear deep into a run; random mid-run events (Golden Hour, Meteor Shower, Amber Storm, Stampede) and rare bonus zones break up the course; every lap adds a twist (storm, fog, eclipse) and pays more; and daily and weekly missions (behind their own Missions button) give a reason to play each day. Every form can ATTACK (a lunge that breaks what its Power allows and bonks wild dinosaurs; Power shortens its cooldown, Speed the slide's), and heroes wear a weapon (it wears out with each hit and is repaired at the Camp with materials) and armor (or a dinosaur card in the weapon slot), crafted at the Crafting Bench from materials found in runs (each craft rolls a rarity, Common to Legendary) and equipped at the Camp, the full-screen place for levelling up and gear (the Market and Field Guide are full screens too). The progression is spread out (dinosaur buttons at 450/1000/1600/2200 m, new hazards every 250–500 m up to ~9400 m) so it takes many runs. The world moves through Forest, Desert, Canyon and Volcanic Badlands zones (1500 m each, `[zones] length_m`; repeating every 6000 m), with a day/night cycle on top (a sun or moon icon by the distance shows the time of day). The keyboard also works for desktop testing. All sound effects and the music are synthesised by a script.

## Configuring progression
**`config/progression.cfg`** is the one file to edit for pacing. It sets dino unlock distances (`[dinos]`), the distance that opens the second booster slot (`[boosters] slot_2`), Market prices in meat (`[market]`) and the best distance that puts each item on sale (`[market_unlocks]`), when each hazard, terrain feature, booster and dino trap is first introduced (`[introductions]`), T-rex charge distances (`[trex]` charge_m, charge_every_m), zone length (`[zones]` length_m), the starter chest and wild-dinosaur preview before the Raptor (`[early]`), and pacing knobs (`[pacing]`: marinate_m, big_clearance_m, intro_solo_spawns, familiar_after_m, gate_spacing_min/max, zone_showcase_m, ramp_start_m/ramp_full_m). `Progression._load()` reads it at startup, falling back to `DEFAULT_*` in `progression.gd` for anything missing. Banner and card text stays in `Progression.INTRO_TEXT`. The header comment is the full unlock schedule. An introduction distance is the *earliest* point: a new hazard still waits for `marinate_m` after the previous new thing and stays `big_clearance_m` clear of the next dino, first zone visit or booster slot, and zone-only hazards also need their zone. Check changes with `tests/timeline_probe.gd` (see [tests/CLAUDE.md](tests/CLAUDE.md)).

**`config/requirements.cfg`** is the dependency tree: what needs what before it happens (amber, events, hazards, power-ups, Market items; e.g. amber needs a dinosaur unlocked). See [docs/systems/requirements.md](docs/systems/requirements.md).

**`config/crafting.cfg`** sets crafting: when the Crafting Bench opens, the 12 materials (rarity, Market bundle price), every weapon and armor recipe, rarity odds and multipliers, catalysts, salvage and repair costs, and what drops materials in runs (broken obstacles, wild dinosaurs, reward spots, chests, fossils). Weapons and armor are crafted, never bought. See [docs/systems/crafting.md](docs/systems/crafting.md).

**`config/hints.cfg`** holds the loading screen's hints, each shown only once the player has reached what it talks about (see [run-loop](docs/systems/run-loop.md#loading-screen-scriptspreloadergd)).

**`config/damage.cfg`** sets what running into each obstacle costs: damage tiers (light ¼, medium ½, heavy 1 heart) and each obstacle's tier; pits, liquids, quicksand, dino gates and being caught stay instant. See [docs/systems/health-and-damage.md](docs/systems/health-and-damage.md).

**`config/obstacle_looks.cfg`** gives each look of a hazard (rock, mushroom, twig, raptor skull, ember rock...) its own NEW banner the first time it's on screen. See [docs/systems/progression.md](docs/systems/progression.md).

**`config/missions.cfg`** sets the daily and weekly missions' counts, rewards and target sizes (behind the main menu's Missions button). See [docs/systems/goals.md](docs/systems/goals.md).

**`config/pickups.cfg`** sets the rules and odds for amber, meat, hearts, power-ups and fossils: what a reward spot holds (`[spots]`, `[counts]`), where spots go (`[placement]`: sky trail, floating islands, staircase, arc, heights), how they get richer deeper in a run (`[escalation]`), what broken obstacles drop (`[break_loot]`), which power-up turns up (`[power_ups]`) and the milestone chests' four rarities (`[chests]`: odds per milestone, meat, XP, reward lists). Its header explains the placement order. Read by `scripts/loot.gd`.

## Layout
- `project.godot`: 1280×720 viewport, `canvas_items` stretch, `expand` aspect (the user's choice, set in the editor and confirmed; it was `keep_height`: wide phones see more world, taller screens see more height, and the HUD lays out from the visible rect; the tests pin `keep_height` because headless windows are square), sensor-landscape orientation, `emulate_touch_from_mouse` (desktop clicks become touches), ETC2/ASTC texture compression.
- `export_presets.cfg`: iOS and Android presets. Android: arm64, package `com.jomari.rokrunner`, name "Rok Runner", version 1.0, no permissions, immersive, launcher and splash icon `assets/icon_1024.png`, export path `../Exported Games/Rok Adventure/Rok Runner.apk`, no Gradle build (fine for a debug APK; a Play Store `.aab` needs Gradle build, the Android build template and the user's own release keystore). This Mac has the Godot 4.7.2 export templates, the Android SDK (`~/Library/Android/sdk`: build-tools 36, platform 37, platform-tools), Java 17 from Homebrew (`openjdk@17`, set as Editor Settings → Export → Android → Java SDK Path `/opt/homebrew/opt/openjdk@17/libexec/openjdk.jdk/Contents/Home`) and the debug keystore (`~/Library/Application Support/Godot/keystores/debug.keystore`, standard `android` / `androiddebugkey`). Build a debug APK with `godot --headless --path . --export-debug "Android" "../Exported Games/Rok Adventure/Rok Runner.apk"` (about 260 MB) and install it with `~/Library/Android/sdk/platform-tools/adb install -r <apk>` (USB debugging on). The app icon and the boot splash are the user's group art (`assets/icon_1024.png`, see [tools/CLAUDE.md](tools/CLAUDE.md)).
- `main.tscn`: a single `Node2D` with `scripts/main.gd`. Everything else is built in code.
- `assets/`: generated, pre-scaled copies of the art in `../Assets`, plus `assets/sounds/` (synthesised). Do not edit them by hand. Regenerate them with `tools/prepare_assets.py` and `tools/make_sounds.py`. Rok's and the four original dinosaurs' art come from the user's polished eight-pose sheets with `@2x` twins (sharper, same on-screen size; `tools/prepare_form_art.py`, loaded by `SpriteLib.tex`); the Market's UI is the user's Marketplace UI pack (`tools/prepare_market_ui.py`); gear icons and the heroes' hand/back sockets for showing equipped weapons come from `tools/prepare_gear.py`; the terrain is the user's seamless pack (`tools/prepare_terrain.py`, the Forest on its seamless strip); the milestone chest is the user's animated chest (`tools/prepare_chest.py`); the jump/slide/attack buttons and the hero/dino/power-up slot rings are the user's art (`tools/prepare_action_buttons.py`, `tools/prepare_slot_frames.py`); the heroes' per-weapon and the dinosaurs' attack clips are the user's attack pack (`tools/prepare_attacks.py`).
- `scripts/`: the game. `tests/`: headless smoke test, autopilot bot and screenshot capture. `tools/`: the asset pipeline.

## Run
- Play: `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path .` (or open `project.godot` in the editor and press F5).
- Tests: `godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 -s tests/run_tests.gd` (must print `ALL TESTS PASSED`).
- After you add a new `class_name` script, run `godot --headless --path . --import` once so the class cache updates.

## UI layout rules (the user's)
Nothing on screen may overlap or spill out of its frame. Check every UI change in a screenshot at 1280×720 **and** a wide phone (`--resolution 1560x720`), not just in tests.
- **Touch controls** (Rok and the four dinosaur buttons, JUMP, SLIDE, ATTACK, the booster slots): no two may touch, measured with what's drawn (a sandstone ring sticks out `TouchButton.FRAME_OUT` past the button), all on screen and clear of Rok's lane. `run_tests.gd` checks this ("no control overlaps another"); when you resize or move a control, or give it a ring or art, change the arc numbers in `tuning.gd` until it passes, and keep that check.
- **Screens with grids** (Market, Camp, Field Guide): cards fill their columns (fixed small gaps, no spread-out dead space between them) and are sized from the space they have (the Market fits two full rows above its details strip, `Market._layout`) instead of a fixed size; a card's picture, name and price sit inside its frame's border (`Market.CARD_INSET`), never on it; long names step a font size down rather than run into neighbours.
- **Text in frames** (details strips, speech bubbles, counters): text stays inside the art's inner area (content margins at least the frame's border), wraps, and scrolls inside a clipping container if it can still be too long; keep the most important lines (name, what it does, price/lock) visible without scrolling.
- **Counters** (meat): sized for the largest value they'll show, with the icon inside the frame's border; a stretchable 9-slice frame means new art isn't needed to make one bigger.

## Conventions
- All gameplay numbers live in `scripts/tuning.gd`. Form stats live in `scripts/forms.gd`.
- No physics engine. Vertical motion and AABB collisions are written by hand in `player.gd` and `main.gd`.
